What makes a meal memorable

Daniel Brooks is a food expert with five years of experience evaluating dining experiences, food quality, flavor balance, presentation, ambiance and hospitality. With a background in culinary arts and food preparation, he looks at what makes a meal memorable, from the quality of its ingredients to the techniques used to prepare and serve it.

He is especially interested in American cuisine, breakfast and brunch, lunch and dinner menus, and the dining experiences that bring people back to a restaurant. His goal is to help readers make informed food choices through practical observations and clear, easy-to-understand reviews.

How I evaluate a meal

Taste is only the start. Every review looks at the whole experience.

Flavor and seasoning

Balance, depth and how well each dish is seasoned.

Texture and aroma

How a dish feels and smells, and whether it is cooked the way it should be.

Cooking methods

The technique behind the plate and how consistently it is done.

Presentation

How the food looks when it arrives and whether it fits the style of the place.

Menu variety

Range, clarity and value across the whole menu.

Service and hospitality

How welcome you feel, from the door to the check.

Ambiance

Everyday meals

Why simple dishes succeed, and where others fall short.
